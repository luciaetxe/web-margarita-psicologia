You do a lot and it feels like little. When you achieve something, you celebrate for a second and you're already thinking about what's next. Resting makes you feel guilty. And inside there's a voice that talks to you the way you'd never talk to anyone else.

That's self-demand. And it almost always comes with anxiety, trouble sleeping, and the feeling of not being enough even when everyone tells you otherwise.

## Where it comes from

Nobody is born pushing themselves like this. We learn it. At home, at school, in relationships where affection seemed to depend on doing everything right. It was a way of adapting and of being loved, and it worked. The problem is that you don't need it anymore and it's still in charge.

It isn't you, it's the programming you grew up with.

## How we work on it in therapy

- First you see it. You learn to catch that voice the moment it shows up, instead of believing it.
- You understand what it protected you from and why it's still there. Once you understand it, it stops being your enemy.
- You set boundaries, with yourself too. Saying no without feeling you've failed.
- You change the way you treat yourself. This is where anxiety really comes down, because you stop living as if you were being tested.
